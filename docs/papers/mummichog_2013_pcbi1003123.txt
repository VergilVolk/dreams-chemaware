    Abstract
     The functional interpretation of high throughput metabolomics by mass spectrometry is hindered by the identification of
     metabolites, a tedious and challenging task. We present a set of computational algorithms which, by leveraging the
     collective power of metabolic pathways and networks, predict functional activity directly from spectral feature tables
     without a priori identification of metabolites. The algorithms were experimentally validated on the activation of innate
     immune cells.

                                                                               Sometimes, a single m/z feature may match to several metabolites
                                                                               in the same module/pathway, inflating the overlap number. Thus,
                                                                               mummichog always compares the number of overlap metabolites
                                                                               and the number of corresponding m/z features, and uses the
                                                                               smaller number for enrichment calculation, since the smaller
                                                                               number is more likely to be true.
