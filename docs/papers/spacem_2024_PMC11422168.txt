Spatial single-cell isotope tracing reveals

heterogeneity of de novo fatty acid synthesis

in cancer

Received: 16 August 2023            Elena Buglakova 1,2, Måns Ekelöf 1, Michaela Schwaiger-Haber 3,4,5,
Accepted: 26 July 2024              Lisa Schlicker 6, Martijn R. Molenaar 1, Mohammed Shahraz1, Lachlan Stuart1,
Published online: 9 September 2024  Andreas Eisenbarth 1, Volker Hilsenstein 1, Gary J. Patti 3,4,5,
                                    Almut Schulze 6 , Marteinn T. Snaebjornsson 6 &
    Check for updates               Theodore Alexandrov 1,7,8,9,10,11

                                    While heterogeneity is a key feature of cancer, understanding metabolic
                                    heterogeneity at the single-cell level remains a challenge. Here we present
                                    13C-SpaceM, a method for spatial single-cell isotope tracing that extends the
                                    previously published SpaceM method with detection of 13C6-glucose-derived
                                    carbons in esterified fatty acids. We validated 13C-SpaceM on spatially
                                    heterogeneous models using liver cancer cells subjected to either
                                    normoxia-hypoxia or ATP citrate lyase depletion. This revealed substantial
                                    single-cell heterogeneity in labelling of the lipogenic acetyl-CoA pool and in
                                    relative fatty acid uptake versus synthesis hidden in bulk analyses. Analysing
                                    tumour-bearing brain tissue from mice fed a 13C6-glucose-containing diet,
                                    we found higher glucose-dependent synthesis of saturated fatty acids and
                                    increased elongation of essential fatty acids in tumours compared with
                                    healthy brains. Furthermore, our analysis uncovered spatial heterogeneity
                                    in lipogenic acetyl-CoA pool labelling in tumours. Our method enhances
                                    spatial probing of metabolic activities in single cells and tissues, providing
                                    insights into fatty acid metabolism in homoeostasis and disease.
