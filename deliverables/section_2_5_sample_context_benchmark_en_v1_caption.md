**Figure X | Benchmarking sample-context biochemical evidence for metabolite annotation.** Left, Top-1 accuracy gains within three matched evaluation settings. Across the six-source development panel, the overall gain was 5.93 percentage points (range across sources, 3.70–8.67). In the complete real-library retrieval benchmark, Top-1 accuracy increased from 75.41% to 78.30% (+2.88 percentage points; formula-cluster 95% confidence interval, +1.55 to +4.39); within 753 negative-mode queries, it increased from 64.41% to 70.65% (+6.24 percentage points). Independent MassBank testing and database-coverage- and degree-matched controls showed that part of the apparent static-network gain was attributable to database inclusion bias. Right, truth-blind yield of candidate-specific biochemical support in two untargeted metabolomics studies. Among 51,976 MS/MS queries, 4,996 received candidate-specific biochemical support, compared with a maximum of 4,072 after 20 metabolite-to-sample permutations and 91 after degree-preserving network rewiring. The observed yield exceeded the permutation maximum by 22.69% overall and by 23.18% and 19.91% in the two studies. These event counts do not measure annotation accuracy: structure truth remained unopened, and the event-concentration criterion required for subsequent ranking evaluation was not met.
